Short answer

Two candidates tie. Nemotron IF-Chat-v1 and Mixture-of-Thoughts are both predicted to cut step time by 3.6% with random pad tokens at truncation 8,192 and 2 sequences per GPU (0.31 s and 0.35 s per step). That is twice Tulu-3's 1.8% at the same truncation; at derisk's 4,096, Tulu-3 is 1.6% predicted and 1.1% measured. WildChat-1M comes third at 2.6%.

Proposed pick: Nemotron IF-Chat-v1, with Mixture-of-Thoughts as the second A/B arm. IF-Chat ties on the predicted gain, is Nemotron 3 Nano's own SFT data, and loses less to truncation at 8,192: 12% of samples are cut, against 24% of Mixture-of-Thoughts (mostly its Codeforces traces, which average 13k tokens).

Why the dataset with the highest EP imbalance does not win

  • With <|im_end|> pads, Tulu-3 (1.62×) and WildChat (1.61×) have the highest EP imbalance. Their heavy-tailed lengths give them the most padding, about 34%. But their micro-batches are small (2.2k and 3.9k tokens per GPU), so the expert work that the imbalance stretches is a small part of each step.
  • IF-Chat and Mixture-of-Thoughts pad less (30% and 28%) but put 9.5k–11.6k tokens in each micro-batch. Each point of imbalance therefore costs them three to four times more time.
  • With 2 sequences per GPU, the padding share cannot exceed 50% whatever the dataset, and all four land at 28–35%. What a dataset really changes is micro-batch size. Even the best natural dataset stays far below derisk-v2's 12% at fixed 8,192 padding, where 91% of tokens were pads.

Things the screen turned up

  • IF-Chat's long samples are its reasoning-off chats (64% of rows, median 3,778 tokens, multi-turn), not its reasoning-on rows (median 1,306). The Nano chat template keeps only the final assistant turn's reasoning, and reasoning-on rows are mostly short instruction-following prompts.
  • IF-Chat does not load in ChatDataset with Nano's stock chat template. About a third of rows are multi-turn with reasoning, and they fail the answer-only loss mask because the template rewrites earlier turns (it drops their <think>). A copy of the template with {% generation %} markers (templates/nano3_generation.jinja) fixes this:
    • On 900 checked conversations it renders identical text.
    • On the 797 conversations the stock path can mask, it produces identical loss masks.
    The IF-Chat A/B would use it.
  • ChatDataset's JSONL reader splits files with str.splitlines(), which also breaks on U+2028, U+2029 and U+0085 inside JSON strings. 32 of the 6,000 WildChat rows contain them, so JSONL written with raw Unicode fails to load. The samples here are written with ASCII escapes. The reader itself is an AutoModel bug and is left for a separate fix.
  • nvidia/Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset-v2, the mix used by AutoModel's Nano-30B example, was not screened. It is gated, and this HF account has not accepted its terms.

How far to trust the predictions

  • The model is calibrated on six derisk runs, all on Tulu-3, and it overpredicts where micro-batches are small. Against the derisk-v2 batchmax runs (Tulu-3 at 4,096, about 2k tokens per micro-batch, per-step paired differences):
    • Random pads: 1.6% predicted vs 1.1% ± 0.4% measured.
    • Masked natural filler: 1.6% predicted vs 0.2% ± 0.3%.
    • Visible natural filler: 3.7% predicted vs 1.0% ± 0.5%.
    IF-Chat's and Mixture-of-Thoughts' micro-batches are 4–5× larger. That is closer to the fixed-8,192 runs, where the model fits within 0.04 s, but expect their realized saving to fall somewhat below 3.6%. The small-batch bias also means the gap to WildChat and Tulu-3 is, if anything, larger than shown.
  • The savings assume warm shape caches, as in derisk-v2's second pass. In a first pass, every new sequence length costs about 1 s of cuDNN plan building (derisk-v1), which dilutes the gain.
  • WildChat's files are ordered by time. Its sample over-represents GPT-4 conversations, which run longer (15% vs 12% for gpt-4-1106-preview), so its prediction is slightly optimistic. The other two samples match their splits to within 0.3 points.

Bigger levers than the dataset

  • Pads the router sees in context. derisk-v2.3b's visible natural filler routed like real text (1.15× imbalance at fixed 8,192). Modeled that way, it would save 7.9% on IF-Chat and 7.8% on Mixture-of-Thoughts at 8,192. That is an upper bound: at Tulu's small micro-batches the same model predicted 3.7% and 1.0% was measured.
  • 4 sequences per GPU at truncation 4,096. The largest micro-batch is then 16k tokens, the same size that fit in derisk. Predicted savings: 3.2% on Tulu-3 and 3.8–4.1% on IF-Chat and WildChat. At 8,192, 4 per GPU predicts 5.5–5.8% on IF-Chat and Mixture-of-Thoughts, but allows 32k-token micro-batches, which ran out of memory in derisk-v1.
  • Keeping pads out of the experts. DeepEP already skips tokens routed to index −1. Doing this would remove the hot spot and the wasted expert work outright, rather than spreading them.

Proposed next step (phase 2, not run)

A/B on IF-Chat and Mixture-of-Thoughts at truncation 8,192 with 2 sequences per GPU: <|im_end|> pads vs random pads, two passes of 20 steps each (derisk-v2's protocol), run one after another. That is 4 runs, about an hour of GPU time.

Compare the arms step by step. Both arms see identical data in identical order, so pairing cuts the standard error 3–5× (0.015–0.020 s instead of about 0.06 s on derisk's batchmax runs). A 3% effect on a roughly 9 s step is then many standard errors from zero.
