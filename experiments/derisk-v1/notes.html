Short answer

Yes, padding changes MFU a lot, but mostly in the metric rather than in useful work. AutoModel's mfu counts pad tokens as work, and it jumps about 10× (1.69% → 16.69%) when every micro-batch is padded to 8,192 tokens: the GPUs process 7.9× more tokens in a similar step time. Counting only real tokens, MFU goes 1.13% → 1.38% over the 20 steps. Once dynamic padding's per-shape caches are warm, it is clearly better: 4.88% effective MFU and 2,482 real tokens/s/GPU, versus 1.38% and 700 with fixed padding (diagnostic).

Why fixed padding looks almost free over these 20 steps

  • Per-shape tax on dynamic padding. TE's cuDNN fused attention builds an execution plan for each new sequence length, which costs about 1.09 s per new length (cut-cross-entropy adds about 66 ms per new token-count bin). With 8 micro-batches per step and almost every length new, the first pass pays roughly 13 s per step: 17.8 s per step in pass 1 versus 4.49 s in pass 2 on the same data. pad-fixed8192 has one shape and pays once.
  • Small micro-batches don't fill an H100. About 2 × 700 real tokens per micro-batch per GPU leaves per-layer fixed costs (DeepEP dispatch/combine synchronization, FSDP collectives, host launch overhead) dominant. Even warm, dynamic padding reaches only 4.9% effective MFU. The 16K-token micro-batches of pad-fixed8192 amortize those costs, which is why its padded MFU is highest.

What the pad tokens do inside the MoE

  • They all take the same path. Pads are the EOS token <|im_end|> with attention mask 0. Attention and Mamba mask them out, so every pad token reaches every MoE layer with the same hidden state, and the router sends all of them to the same 6 experts: 100% of pad assignments per step, versus about 10% on the top 6 for real tokens. The exact expert IDs are in the per-layer tables below.
  • They are really computed. Nemotron V3 does not pass its padding mask to the MoE layers (the MoE layer supports masking; the model just doesn't wire it up). In pad-fixed8192, about 91% of all routed-expert work therefore lands on 6 experts per layer, that is on the one to six GPUs hosting them.
  • The GPUs hosting those experts become stragglers. Expert-parallel imbalance (max ÷ mean assignments per GPU in a dispatch) averages about 2.8× with fixed padding versus about 1.7× with dynamic padding (worst dispatch about 5.2× versus about 2.9×). The hottest GPU peaks at 67.9 GiB, and with 4 sequences per GPU it ran out of memory inside the grouped expert GEMM.
  • Measured in a trace. In a 2-step Nsight Systems sample of pad-fixed8192, 33–38% of GPU kernel time on ranks 0 and 1 is DeepEP's cached_notify_combine, a synchronization kernel that mostly waits for the slowest GPU in each MoE dispatch (Nsight handoff).
  • Rough cost of the hot spot (estimate). Routed experts are 42.6% of the model's FLOPs per token. If each dispatch waits for the most-loaded GPU, a 2.8× imbalance stretches that phase about 2.8×, so the step takes about 1.75× as long as perfectly balanced routing would.

What to do about it

  • Don't use AutoModel's mfu as an efficiency number when padding is heavy. Log effective MFU or real tokens/s next to it (both are in this report and in wandb).
  • Keep pads out of the experts. Wiring padding_mask into Nemotron V3's MoE layers would remove both the wasted expert FLOPs and the EP hot spot; DeepEP already drops tokens routed to index −1. Packing (THD) avoids pads altogether. Both need their own derisk runs.
  • If you keep dynamic padding with TE/cuDNN attention, bound the number of distinct shapes, for example by rounding lengths up to a multiple of 64–256 with pad_seq_len_divisible or bucketing by length. Otherwise every new length costs about 1 s until the cache fills.
  • Next, profile further with profile_nsys.sh: capture pad-batchmax to split its per-step host time from GPU time, and capture all 8 ranks of pad-fixed8192 to see the hot GPUs' side of the DeepEP wait.

Caveats

  • 20 steps, one seed, one node. Warmup steps 0–1 are excluded from all averages.
  • The software stack deviates from the lockfile: CUDA 12.8 torch, and DeepEP built intranode-only with aggressive PTX instructions disabled (see Setup). Absolute MFU is low for both arms and may be higher on the reference container. Both arms used the same stack.
  • The warm pass repeats identical shapes, so it is the best case for dynamic padding. A real run revisits lengths gradually as its caches fill.
  • The padded FLOPs formula overstates executed work slightly for pads: TE skips attention for masked positions, and attention is about 3% of the formula's FLOPs at 700-token sequences and about 7% at 8,192.
