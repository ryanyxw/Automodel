Short answer

Evening out the pad-token load across GPUs raises MFU, but only where the expert hot spot is the bottleneck. On pad-fixed8192 (91.5% padding), the best variant, v2.3b (natural-text filler visible to the model), cut the step time from 15.98 s to 10.59 s (−34%) and raised effective MFU from 1.38% to 2.08% (+51%). The v2.2 expert placement gave −25% / +33%, and random or masked natural filler (v2.1, v2.3a) about −12 to −14%. On pad-batchmax, no variant changed the step time by more than about 2%, in either pass.

What each variant did to the load (pad-fixed8192)

  • v2.1 random pad tokens and v2.3a natural pad, masked behave the same. With attention mask 0, attention and Mamba skip the filler, so each filler token reaches the router with only its own embedding. The router still favors a narrow set of experts for such context-free tokens: the top 6 experts per layer take 58–59% of filler assignments (100% with <|im_end|> pads, 10.6% for real tokens). Mean EP imbalance 2.78× → about 2.1×.
  • v2.2 expert placement leaves the pads alone and moves experts instead, so each GPU hosts at most one pad-heavy expert per layer. Mean imbalance 2.78× → 1.38× (predicted 1.30×), worst dispatch 5.09× → 1.88×. With 6 hot experts on 8 GPUs and no replication, about 1.33× is the floor for the pad share of the load.
  • v2.3b natural pad, visible gives the filler attention mask 1, so it is read like an extra, unsupervised document. Filler tokens get real context and route like real text (top-6 share 10.2%), mean imbalance drops to 1.15× (worst 1.38×), and the hottest GPU's peak memory falls from 67.9 to 62.2 GiB. The model now also runs attention over the filler, and it is still the fastest variant.
  • All variants leave the loss on real tokens unchanged (max |Δloss| ≤ 0.0013): padding sits after the real tokens and the model is causal, so real tokens never see the filler, and the filler has label −100.

Why pad-batchmax doesn't move

  • Pads are only 33% of its tokens, so the hot spot is milder to begin with (1.68×).
  • Its step time is dominated by other costs: in pass 1, building a cuDNN attention plan for each new sequence length (derisk-v1); in pass 2, the fixed per-layer costs of small micro-batches. Even near-perfect balance (v2.2, 1.12×) or real-text routing (v2.3b, 1.19×) removes little of either.

Next steps

  • v2.3b is sequence packing with a throwaway document. Packing real training data into those positions would make the same compute useful instead of discarded.
  • Alternatively, drop pads from the experts entirely by passing the padding mask into Nemotron V3's MoE layers; that removes the wasted expert work as well as the hot spot, without the extra attention.
  • A production version of v2.2 would rebalance online from recent router counts (here it was tuned on derisk-v1's counts for the same data order) and could replicate the hottest experts to get below the 1.33× floor.
